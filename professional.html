Research

01 · Solar physics · 2022–present

Magneto-acoustic-gravity Waves

Since 2022, I have worked with Prof. Jason Jackiewicz at New Mexico State University, investigating the propagation of atmospheric gravity waves in the lower solar atmosphere, and their coupling and conversion into other magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) wave modes. My research combines radiative three-dimensional MHD simulations, multiwavelength observations, and classical theoretical calculations to better understand these processes. This work has provided a first of it's kind characterization and tracking of gravity-wave propagation properties and mode conversion/reflection in the lower solar atmosphere, offering a new framework for understanding the potential role of gravity waves in heating the solar chromosphere, particularly through their interactions with slow magnetoacoustic and Alfvén waves. You can learn more about my work on magneto-acoustic-gravity waves here!

Pre-main-sequence Accretion

My journey in astronomy research began as an undergraduate at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where I worked with Prof. Katherine B. Follette (Amherst College) studying magnetospheric accretion onto T-Tauri and Herbig Ae/Be stars in transitional disk systems in the Giant Accreting Protoplanet Survey. My senior thesis examined second-to-minute Hα variability in these systems, and I continued that analysis as a post-baccalaureate researcher at Amherst College. Research workflow development

AstroStack

I’m developing AstroStack, a Mac app for students and researchers who want to organize scientific papers, notes, figures, and data in one workspace. It connects with Zotero and arXiv to help users build their library and keep up with new publications. Users can read and search PDFs, highlight passages, write notes with equations, and ask AI tools questions about a paper. A figure gallery collects images from publications, while a plot digitizer turns published graphs into numerical data for visualization and export. For astronomy research, it also provides access to data archives and tools for organizing proposals and observing plans. Customizable themes and Apple Shortcuts support let users adapt the workspace and automate routine tasks.

Apple Shortcuts

Six shortcuts for planning your day, capturing work and keeping in touch. These sharing copies include setup questions and a numbered checklist in the opening Comment. Open a shortcut in the Shortcuts editor to read that checklist before its first run.

Apple Intelligence is optional. Morning Routine, Nighttime Routine, Tasks and Quick Note offer a ChatGPT browser handoff for devices without native AI support; Agenda’s alternative image route uses your own SSH/Codex server. The browser handoff asks you to paste the prompt into ChatGPT and return its answer. Each download explains its account and permission requirements.

Install the helpers first

Some shortcuts call separate helpers. Add the ones listed for your shortcut, read their opening Comments, then select them in the main shortcut’s setup questions. I’m Late runs on its own.

  • Availability — Sharing: copies free-time text for Agenda.
  • Success Sound — Sharing: completion cue for Agenda, Tasks and Quick Note.
  • When Do I Need To Leave By? — Sharing: departure estimate for Morning Routine and the bedtime helper. Choose its source calendar manually.
  • Calculate Ideal Bedtime — Sharing: bedtime for Nighttime Routine; connect the departure helper and choose preparation/sleep durations.
  • Ask ChatGPT — Sharing: browser handoff for Morning Routine, Nighttime Routine, Tasks and Quick Note.

Read each checklist for manual calendar selection, server/account configuration and optional customization. Setup questions were previewed on a Mac; fresh-device imports and full account-dependent runs remain unverified.
