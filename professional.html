Research

01 · Solar physics · 2022–present

Magneto-acoustic-gravity Waves

Since 2022, I have worked with Prof. Jason Jackiewicz at New Mexico State University, investigating the propagation of atmospheric gravity waves in the lower solar atmosphere, and their coupling and conversion into other magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) wave modes. My research combines radiative three-dimensional MHD simulations, multiwavelength observations, and classical theoretical calculations to better understand these processes. This work has provided a first of it's kind characterization and tracking of gravity-wave propagation properties and mode conversion/reflection in the lower solar atmosphere, offering a new framework for understanding the potential role of gravity waves in heating the solar chromosphere, particularly through their interactions with slow magnetoacoustic and Alfvén waves. You can learn more about my work on magneto-acoustic-gravity waves here!

02 · Stellar Astrophysics · 2021–2022

Pre-main-sequence Accretion

My journey in astronomy research began as an undergraduate at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where I worked with Prof. Katherine B. Follette (Amherst College) studying magnetospheric accretion onto T-Tauri and Herbig Ae/Be stars in transitional disk systems in the Giant Accreting Protoplanet Survey. My senior thesis examined second-to-minute Hα variability in these systems, and I continued that analysis as a post-baccalaureate researcher at Amherst College. Instruction, research readiness & mentorship

Teaching & Mentorship

Sharing my knowledge of the universe with curious minds has been one of the greatest joys of my journey toward becoming a researcher. For the past decade, I have had the pleasure of teaching and mentoring students from a wide range of backgrounds, abilities, and learning styles across several STEM disciplines, including Physics, Astronomy, Mathematics, and Computer Science. I firmly believe that every child in our communities deserves access to high-quality STEM education, not only to pursue their own scientific interests but also to become scientifically and numerically literate citizens. Through my experiences working with students, including those from groups historically underrepresented in STEM, I have seen firsthand the profound impact that education, mentorship, and guidance can have on a student's academic and personal growth.
